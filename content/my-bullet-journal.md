Below is **your Bullet Journal book** — not a reinterpretation of the original method, not a productivity manifesto, and not something you need to keep tweaking.

This is a **working manual** for how _you_ already behave, with guardrails to stop task rot and perfectionism.

You can copy this into a note, print it, or rewrite it by hand.  
Once written, **don’t keep improving it**. Use it.

# The Raghu Journal

_A decision-first journal for daily execution and yearly reflection_

---

## 1. What this journal is for (scope)

This journal exists to do **three things only**:

1. Capture what is on my mind
    
2. Decide what deserves action
    
3. Leave evidence for reflection (monthly → yearly)
    

This journal is **not**:

- A motivation system
    
- A habit tracker
    
- A planning tool for distant futures
    
- A place where all ideas must become tasks
    

If something doesn’t fit these three goals, it goes elsewhere or nowhere.

---

## 2. Core rules (non-negotiable)

Write these at the front.

1. **If I can’t act on it soon, it is not automatically a task**
    
2. **Tasks may live in daily logs for a maximum of ~3 days**
    
3. **If a task matters, it must return to a day**
    
4. **Striking out a task is success, not failure**
    
5. **Thoughts and tasks are different containers**
    

These rules matter more than layouts.

---

## 3. Structure of the journal

### A. Index

Standard. Nothing special.

---

### B. Parking Lot (Someday / Maybe)

**Purpose:**  
Ideas with no urgency or commitment.

**What goes here:**

- “Learn Go”
    
- “Contribute to open source”
    
- “Write long-form blog”
    

**Rules:**

- Never copied into daily logs directly
    
- Only re-enter the system when converted into a _concrete next action_
    
- No review schedule required
    

This page protects your ambition without polluting execution.

---

### C. Monthly Rituals Page (KEEP THIS)

**Purpose:**  
Prevent missed obligations.

**What goes here:**

- Bills
    
- Recharges
    
- Transfers
    
- Recurring admin
    

This page already works. Do not optimize it.

---

### D. Decision Page (one per month)

This replaces “monthly tasks”.

**Title clearly:**

```
MAY – DECISION PAGE
```

**Purpose:**  
A holding pen for tasks that survived daily logs but haven’t earned a date.

**What is allowed here:**

- Clear actions
    
- Intended this month
    
- Not doable today
    

**What is NOT allowed here:**

- Vague goals
    
- Thinking topics
    
- Long-term aspirations
    

**Lifecycle of a task here:**

- Pulled into a daily log → crossed out here
    
- Scheduled elsewhere → crossed out here
    
- Parked → crossed out here
    
- Killed → crossed out here
    

No task moves to the next month automatically.

---

### E. Daily Logs (your primary workspace)

**When used:** Morning only.

**Flow (unchanged, with one addition):**

1. Look at yesterday
    
2. Copy tasks you still intend to do
    
3. Glance at Decision Page
    
4. Pull **at most one** task from it (if relevant)
    
5. Write today’s list
    

No evening review required.

---

## 4. The 3-Day Survival Rule (precise)

A task may appear in daily logs **up to 3 times**.

You do not count days.  
You notice repetition.

When you catch yourself copying a task _again_:

- **Stop**
    
- Move it to the Decision Page
    
- Do **not** copy it to today
    

This is the enforcement mechanism.

---

## 5. Handling “I can’t do this today” thoughts

When something occurs to you, ask **in order**:

### Question 1

**Can I physically act on this in the next 24–48 hours?**

- Yes → daily log
    
- No → Question 2
    

### Question 2

**Is the next action clear and do I intend to do it this month?**

- Yes → Decision Page
    
- No → Question 3
    

### Question 3

**Is this an idea or something I need clarity on?**

- Yes → Thinking Collection
    
- No → Parking Lot
    

There is no fourth option.

---

## 6. Thinking Collections (true journaling)

These are **write-only** until clarity emerges.

**Examples:**

- Job change clarity
    
- Learning topics
    
- Personal decisions
    

**Rules:**

- No task migration from here unless you write a _specific action_
    
- No obligation to review
    
- This is where ambiguity is allowed
    

A thought becomes a task **only when it becomes concrete**.

---

## 7. End of Month (15 minutes max)

This is not planning. This is judgment.

### Steps:

1. Review Decision Page
    
2. For each remaining item:
    
    - Schedule it
        
    - Park it
        
    - Strike it out
        
3. Write a short monthly reflection:
    
    - What mattered?
        
    - What drained me?
        
    - What repeated?
        

Do **not** rewrite tasks into the new month.

---

## 8. Weekly anchor (optional but recommended)

Once a week, on any day:

Write one line:

```
This week only matters if:
– Work:
– Life:
```

That’s it.  
No review, no checklist, no guilt.

Optionally track:

```
Weeks with anchor: __ / 52
```

---

## 9. Yearly review (why this works)

Because:

- Daily logs show effort
    
- Decision Pages show avoidance vs intent
    
- Monthly reflections show patterns
    
- Struck-out tasks show clarity gained
    

Your yearly review is now **evidence-based**, not memory-based.

---

## 10. Anti-perfectionism safeguards

If you catch yourself wanting to:

- Add symbols
    
- Redesign layouts
    
- Track consistency obsessively
    
- Rewrite this guide
    

Do this instead:

- Strike out one task you’ve been avoiding
    

That is progress.

Use this system for **one quarter** without modification.  
Only then decide what to change.
